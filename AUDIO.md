# Vocalization quizzes

Choose **Sound · vocalizations** in Practice by. Site, family, lifer, question-count, and regular/review controls work as in visual practice. All families uses distinct species; a selected family can repeat a species with different recording IDs. Review asks once per species. The default remains 20 questions, capped to available media. The site dropdown retains its all-family target counts; the pool text reports actual audio coverage for the selected filters and the breakdown of geographic match tiers.

The player has no autoplay. Press Play before scoring an answer; replay and seeking do not create attempts. The quiz accepts the same common/scientific names and fuzzy spelling as visual mode. Contributor, species, locality, date/type when catalogued, source link, selection reason, and subspecies caveats appear after answering. Numeric media URLs are present for playback but no identifying metadata is intentionally rendered beforehand. Navigation pauses playback. Results offer playable recordings and credits. Media failures can retry alternatives in the selected preference tier; unclear or unavailable recordings can be skipped without scoring. A failed or skipped question adds neither a success nor a miss. Revealed answers count as misses.

## Separate statistics and history

Visual practice keeps the original `colombia-field-quiz:progress:v1` localStorage key unchanged. Audio uses `colombia-field-quiz:audio-progress:v1`. Each independently retains the most recent 20 scored attempts per species. There is no copying or migration of visual outcomes into audio. Each medium has its own strict greater-than-25% mistake-review pool. Same-settings repetition and immediate quiz-only review retain their medium, scopes, and missed species. Fresh media, when available, stay within the best current location tier. Start over resets quiz settings without deleting either history.

The Statistics for selector switches visual/audio site summaries, family summaries, and linked per-species pages. New species links carry the medium explicitly (`#species/audio/family/Furnariidae`); older three-part links remain supported. Launching from a linked statistics list uses that list's medium. Taxonomic, alphabetical, and accuracy sorting work as before. Histories remain browser/device-local; shared photo review storage is independent.

Rates divide correct by scored attempts, not species-average rates. Site statistics use target-species membership from the checklist, including answers made in other scopes. A shared species contributes to each applicable site. Audio site rates combine recording localities and do not establish mastery of the site’s dialect. Family rates cover all sites. Skips and unavailable media are excluded. Storage failure for one medium does not disable the other.

The **Site-only quizzes** display switch instead uses a separate audio history for that exact site with All families selected. Family/all-sites sessions do not enter it, and it starts with the context-tracking update because prior answers have no site provenance. Site-scoped mistake reviews qualify; each site/species has its own last-20 window. Both site views are recorded independently of the display switch; family rates and recent-mistake eligibility keep using general audio history. The optional per-question checklist-site clue in family/all-sites quizzes is independent of recording locality and does not alter audio selection.

## Geographic selection and limitations

`audio.mjs` ranks current catalog records: explicitly confirmed site (reserved for independently verified mappings), named site-area match, same department, Colombia, neighboring country, then distant fallback. No initial record is asserted to have a confirmed site/subspecies assignment. Only the best available geographic tier enters a scope's question pool; random selection cannot displace a nearby clip with a distant one. These preferences search this quiz’s saved catalog, not the full Macaulay archive.

Named-area matches are conservative string matches within Colombia. Department preferences are broad approximations from the itinerary: Bogotá/Cundinamarca, Caldas, Antioquia, Risaralda/Chocó, or Valle del Cauca as applicable. A department can span different mountain slopes, habitats, and populations. Montezuma's high/mid scopes currently share a general-area preference; clip elevation is not matched. Bellavista's location remains unresolved, so no department or exact-area match is asserted for it. Its recordings use explicit country/broader fallbacks. An all-sites quiz spans several regions and favors Colombian recordings without asserting a single population.

Site subspecies assignments and recording-to-site subspecies matches are not verified in this release. Missing subspecies metadata is reported as unknown, never inferred from proximity. Best-effort fallbacks, especially non-Colombian or breeding-ground recordings of migrants, may differ substantially from vocalizations encountered on the trip. One clip may represent only a call, one song type, or one sex/age. Background species and silence can occur. Catalog identity/credit and audio MIME metadata are checked; every clip has not been auditioned for foreground clarity. Pure mechanical tracks are excluded when explicitly identified in source metadata. The user-facing explanation covers these limits and the player offers an unscored skip.

## Catalog and attribution

The initial catalog contains 674 recording records for 188 of 849 checklist species; 84 species have Colombian records. Coverage is incomplete and shown in setup. Metadata comes only from accessible Macaulay audio catalog entries, their asset-page metadata, and Cornell's public [Guide to Bird Sounds track list](https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/guide-to-bird-sounds/track-list/). Each recording retains its own provenance URL, recordist, locality, canonical Macaulay link, public playback URL, and verification date. Overseas examples from the guide are labeled as broad fallbacks, not local recordings. Sound type and date are left unknown where not available in the retrieved metadata. No audio binaries are downloaded, bundled, rehosted, or put in shared storage; the native player streams Cornell's public MP3 endpoint. Header checks verified `audio/*` MIME types. One inaccessible candidate was excluded.

Macaulay's catalog automated-access restrictions prevented a complete archive search. The importer does not solve or bypass those challenges; it uses accessible public metadata and indexed catalog results. `scripts/prepare-audio-catalog.py` accepts a metadata-cache directory, reconciles exact scientific/common labels against the checklist, excludes known non-vocal tracks, validates asset identities and contributor metadata, and emits `dist/audio.json`. It retains multiple localities instead of filling the catalog with one area's examples. Cached inputs contain public metadata only and are not needed at runtime. Refreshing or expanding this saved audio catalog currently requires rebuilding/deploying the Worker; shared photo annotations continue to update without code deployment.

Source audio and recordings remain credited to their contributors and Cornell Lab / Macaulay Library. No ownership of those recordings or their source identifications is asserted. See Cornell's [media use and embedding guidance](https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48001064551-using-and-requesting-media).

## Validation

Run `node test.mjs`, `node test-audio.mjs`, `node test-app-audio.mjs`, `node scripts/build-worker.mjs`, then `node test-review.mjs` (Node 24+). Pure tests cover every site/family/lifer intersection, geographic preference, catalog provenance, question caps, distinct-recording repeats, isolated histories/review/statistics, and invalid catalogs. The app integration harness drives DOM and media events for playback gating, metadata hiding, audio/visual scoring isolation, correct/missed/revealed answers, results, repeats, review, failed media, reset, and linked scopes. It does not use a browser or screen capture and cannot verify the native controls' visual appearance or audible output. Live HTTP checks verify deployed assets, catalog counts, streaming headers, and preservation of shared photo-review data. Browser/device playback QA remains for the user to check.
